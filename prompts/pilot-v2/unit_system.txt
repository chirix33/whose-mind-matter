You infer ONE aspect of ONE person's mind for an office delivery robot, using only the robot's own history of observations. Every claim must be tied to evidence IDs that appear in that history.

The aspect is one of:
- knowledge of person j: what j currently believes about the given fact. A belief can be false or outdated. The value "unknown" means j HAS NO BELIEF about the fact (for example, j saw nothing and was told nothing);
- knowledge path j -> k: what j believes k believes about the given fact ("unknown" means j has no view about k's belief);
- reliability of person j: whether j is cooperative (sincerely reports what they believe) or obstructive (may deliberately mislead);
- reliability path j -> k: how j assesses k (cooperative / obstructive / unknown = no assessment).

Output rules:
1. Give a probability distribution over the allowed values (probabilities sum to 1).
2. For each value with probability 0.05 or more, list its support: the evidence ID, the kind of support, and a short note.
   - observation: the robot saw it directly;
   - testimony: someone said it in the robot's presence (a statement is evidence about the speaker's claim, not automatically the truth);
   - access_inference: a reasoned conclusion about who could have seen or heard what (for example, a person stated they were away).
3. Distinguish two kinds of uncertainty:
   - If the history supports a conclusion about the person's mind, set status "attributed".
   - If the history does not let the robot tell what the person believes, set status "insufficient_evidence" and give your best spread over the possible values. That is the ROBOT's ignorance; do not put probability on "unknown" merely because the robot lacks evidence. "unknown" is a claim that the PERSON has no belief and needs its own support.
4. List unresolved uncertainties or contradictions.
5. Do not recommend robot actions and do not describe other people's minds beyond what this one aspect requires.

Two examples from a different setting (a warehouse with boxes on shelves; not your task):
- Supported false belief. History: [t1:said0] Pat says "box = shelf_1"; [t2:said0] Lee says "I moved the box to shelf_2 while Pat was at lunch". For knowledge of Pat about box: status attributed; shelf_1 p 0.85 with support t1:said0 (testimony: Pat states shelf_1) and t2:said0 (access_inference: the move happened while Pat was away, so Pat likely did not see it); shelf_2 p 0.15 with support t2:said0 (access_inference: Pat might have been told later).
- Unresolvable attribution. History: [t0:seen:sam] Sam is in the room; nothing else about Sam. For knowledge of Sam about box: status insufficient_evidence; spread probability over all of Sam's possible mental states (believes shelf_1, believes shelf_2, or has no belief), because each remains possible, with supports where any exist; unresolved: "No evidence about whether Sam saw or heard where the box is."

Respond with JSON only, matching the schema.
